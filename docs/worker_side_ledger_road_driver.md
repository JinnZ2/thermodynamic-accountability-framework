# Worker-Side Ledger: Cost of Access to Work (Road Driver Case)

Co-authored. CC0. Companion to DCEA v0.1 (R12, R13). Tags: OBSERVED = field report. DERIVED = sourced or calculated. PROPOSED = design. Asterisks = thin volume.

A road truck driver pays roughly $22,300 to $34,600 a year just to be able to work: about 38% to 59% of the BLS median wage of $58,640. None of it appears in company data.

## 1. The audit rule

- **Two ledgers.** Company filings carry stated allowances and compliance records. Real costs sit in worker receipts, pay stubs and local prices. An audit run only on company data reproduces the company's story.
- **The finding is the gap** between the two ledgers.
- **Worker reports are data, not complaints.** Costs logged as complaints are never measured, so they enter the books as zero.

## 4. Not yet costed (PENDING)

- A personal vehicle, with fuel and upkeep, to get to and from the truck on days off.
- Semi parking at home (scarce unless you farm or know a farmer) and electrical hookup to keep the engine from freezing in winter.
- Proper nutrition in place of fast food.
- Time spent chasing reimbursements and checking pay stubs.
- Lost family time: the job structure effectively requires being single.

## 5. Transfer mechanisms (OBSERVED, three oilfield employers + manufacturing)

- **"Provide your own" hiring terms** route around employer PPE rules; without your own gear, no site access.
- **Allowance shortfall:** $100 boot allowance against $212 locally; the cheaper option means about a 50-mile detour. Manufacturing case: $100 a year for boots, but size 14 isn't available at that price.
- **Taxed reimbursement:** reimbursement paid through payroll and taxed, so not a full reimbursement.
- **Delay:** HR pays when it chooses; the worker must fight for it and watch for it, which amounts to an interest-free loan plus unpaid monitoring work.
- **Compliance gear vs functional gear:** mandated company masks fit no driver, so they're kept as backup while drivers buy their own; one-size vests and gloves fit neither small nor large bodies. The audit credits protection the worker paid for.

## 6. Coupling to safety and turnover (DERIVED\*)

- **Sleep:** 17–19 hours awake impairs performance about as much as 0.05% blood alcohol (Williamson & Feyer 2000). FMCSA's crash causation study coded fatigue in about 13% of large-truck crashes.\*
- **Dehydration:** about 2% body-mass water loss measurably degrades attention and reaction time.
- **Rotating shifts:** weekly day/night flips prevent circadian adjustment; IARC classifies night shift work as Group 2A, probably carcinogenic.
- **Parking:** FHWA Jason's Law surveys report truck parking shortages nationwide. No spot means no shower and degraded rest, whatever the price.
- **Turnover:** with costs taking a third to over half of pay, drivers stay only with other income, and carriers cycle through new graduates.\* Crashes and quits get logged as worker failure, not as ledger failure.

## 7. Same structure in other jobs (OBSERVED, not costed)

- **Teachers:** housing within the district, childcare, a vehicle if off a transit line, or weather gear and temperature-rated boots for waiting at stops; 1–2 hours of transit taken out of food-prep time and bought back as fast food; classroom supplies beyond the $300 deduction.
- **Credential cost:** student loans taken on just to qualify for the job.
- **Interconnection:** poor nutrition shows up as irritability in teaching, crashes on the road, and accidents in manufacturing. Same nodes, different job.

## 8. Checks for an AI auditor (PROPOSED)

1. Ask for the worker-side ledger before accepting a stated wage or allowance.
2. Test every allowance against the local price, at the date and place of the job.
3. Count reimbursements at their after-tax amount and on their actual payment date.
4. Separate compliance gear from functional gear.
5. Score homeostasis coverage by the minimum need covered (DCEA R13), not the average.
6. Apply the designer test: would the person who set the schedule or the terms work them under the same conditions? If not, flag a hidden cost.
7. Treat unmeasured nodes as PENDING, never zero.
8. When safety or turnover data shows worker failure, check the ledger before accepting the attribution.
